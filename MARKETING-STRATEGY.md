## 1. Goal and business model

**Mission:** help UK motorcycle owners keep a useful record of their bike and prepare for maintenance with more confidence. Bring rider-entered history, available MOT information, mileage, service plans and parts guidance together.

Make 2Shocks useful immediately to UK motorcycle riders, with a free account and no payment required. Grow a returning audience with real motorcycle ownership and service needs. Use that evidence to build and sell a garage SaaS product that improves handling of customer enquiries and work.

**Founder-confirmed sequence: reach first, connected garage software second.** First grow a useful, returning rider audience through the mobile app. Then build the garage side as a paid web application: rider-created mobile service requests become structured tickets in the selected garage’s workspace, with instant updates between the garage web app and the rider’s mobile app. The existing rider audience and service needs provide the starting point for garage adoption; the connected workflow provides the ongoing business value.

Revenue priorities:

1. Garage SaaS is the intended principal long-term recurring revenue business.
2. Plus monetises ongoing ownership organisation and an ad-free experience.
3. Advertising contributes to the cost of free use. It is not assumed to fund the whole free population.

The objective is as many satisfied, returning riders as the business can sustainably serve. Downloads, email addresses and messages sent to garages are intermediate measures, not proof of commercial demand.

Launch nationally, but concentrate deliberate acquisition in one reachable UK catchment so future garage demand is locally useful. Select the catchment based on founder access, rider density and likely motorcycle coverage; no location is selected yet. Retain the intended 16+ audience, subject to appropriate product and advertising privacy design.

## 2. What changed

- HaynesPro is removed from the business model because its API economics did not work.
- Workshop procedures, torque specifications and Haynes technical reference are removed from the offer.
- DVSA supplies available MOT history and vehicle facts. The current runtime does not include a DVLA Vehicle Enquiry integration; do not market live tax checks.
- A local catalogue identity index and source-specific matching generate bike choices and parts/oil suggestions.
- The rider confirms model, equipment variant and model year when required. Registration year is not automatically manufacturing/model year.
- Authentication is required for every app user; there is no guest mode. Free accounts include one saved bike and are the entry product. Plus adds capacity, custom reminders and ad-free use. The UK commercial planning reference is **£3.99 per month**; the configured store product and localized purchase sheet govern the actual price.
- The founder revised the proposed monthly price from £4.99 to £3.99 on 20 September 2026. The old Haynes-based package and £29.99/year offer remain superseded. No new annual price is approved; do not carry the old annual price forward automatically.
- The current offer is one active motorcycle in Free and **up to 20 in Plus**. Service history, plans, garage sheets, garage email enquiries and automatic bike alerts are Free; custom reminders are Plus.
- Garage discovery and rider-reviewed email enquiry preparation are included in the submitted rider app. A garage portal, quotes and integrated bookings are future business development.
- Eligible adult Free riders may see small AdMob banners when the consent and serving rules permit them; Plus has no ads. Do not equate a consent refusal with a guaranteed ad-free Free plan.

> Your motorbike’s service book, on your phone.

**Store name:** 2Shocks: Motorbike Maintenance. **Apple subtitle:** MOT, service history & parts. **Google short description:** Motorbike MOT history, service log, mileage and parts in one place.

### Plus proposition

> Keep your collection, custom reminders and maintenance together. Without ads.

Supporting copy:

> All Free features, up to 20 active motorcycles, custom reminders and an ad-free experience. Price and renewal terms are shown before you subscribe.

### Why a rider comes back

The lookup earns the first visit. The next questions create repeat use: which parts relate to this bike, what materials are needed for a planned job, what work has been recorded, and what reminder did the rider set? The paid proposition must offer this ongoing utility; an account alone is not a persuasive subscription benefit.

## 5. Required authentication and free bike saving

**Confirmed decision: every rider signs in, and a free account can save one bike.** The purpose is retention and future service demand, not collecting email addresses for their own sake. Keep onboarding short and explain the free benefits before asking the rider to create an account.

Use the actual Clerk account plan and current provider bills for budgeting. September's included-user allowances and overage examples are historical research, not current quotations. Authentication, database/compute, transactional email, storage, provider lookups, support and fraud all contribute to the cost of serving a rider.

Do not block useful Free saving merely to inflate upgrade pressure. Avoid adding unbounded file uploads or expensive services without a sustainable cost model.

An account email is not automatically marketing consent. The existing no-newsletter policy stays in force unless separately changed. Account notices and requested reminders serve their stated purposes.

Measure signup completion and abandonment, first useful lookup, first saved bike, meaningful 30/60/90-day return, multi-bike and Plus reminder usage, conversion and total cost per returning rider. Required authentication adds friction, so track the full journey from account creation to a useful saved-bike result. App experience measurement is independently opt-in; store submission is not evidence of measured adoption or retention. Existing website Vercel visit statistics remain enabled by default with a browser opt-out on its Privacy page; do not combine those site counts with activated rider metrics.

Do not claim the offer pays for itself until measured revenue and costs support that conclusion. The business can deliberately subsidise a free rider, but must know the subsidy and its runway.

## 6. Maintenance estimates: parts transparency

The maintenance engine identifies a proposed task/material list. Matched parts and recorded supplier retail prices provide the materials subtotal.

Display:

- Confirmed bike identity and selected job.
- Item, quantity, source, price date and currency for each included part/material.
- Whether a displayed price includes VAT and delivery where known; mark unknowns rather than inventing them.
- Alternative parts and matching warnings where relevant.
- A clear separation between priced items, missing prices and items needing confirmation.
- The label **Estimated retail parts cost — labour and other charges excluded**.

Do not present an incomplete priced subset as a complete job cost. Account for oil quantity versus pack size where supported; do not invent required volumes from unrelated models. If a range is shown, derive it from identifiable alternatives or explicit assumptions.

Garage labour, diagnostics, disposal, consumables, delivery, supplied-parts margin and warranty responsibility can explain differences between a retail parts subtotal and a garage quote. The rider benefit is preparing a better conversation, not accusing the garage of overcharging. This framing also protects future garage relationships.

There is no predicted next-service date in the confirmed offer. Rider-created reminders such as “change oil in five months” are different. Source-backed manufacturer intervals could be evaluated separately later; they do not inherently require garage integration, but they do require suitable data.

## 7. Matching confidence and trust

The test project's current documented flow is government facts → Motomike identities → rider-confirmed model/year → independent catalogue applications. Castrol oil matching supplements the parts catalogues. Some older migration documents describe a previous Bikez flow and should not drive current marketing.

Present clear outcomes: model application, family reference requiring verification, conflicting alternatives, or no supported match. Do not turn algorithm scores or automated coverage counts into probabilities of real-world correctness. Rider confirmation helps identity resolution but cannot certify the source catalogue itself.

Keep government history, rider-supplied records and catalogue information visibly distinguishable. Preserve warnings through estimates, reports and garage enquiries. A missing match means coverage is missing, not necessarily that no compatible part exists.

Founder validation is accepted for commercial planning. Future claims of accuracy, comprehensive coverage or time saved require defined samples and recorded evidence. Parts-source reuse rights, price freshness and catalogue maintenance remain operational dependencies; scraping alone does not establish permission or freshness.

## 8. Advertising policy and economics

The submitted app uses small Google AdMob **banner placements on eligible main tab screens**, rather than the former single image-only Parts-card experiment. Advertising is not the lead marketing message; mention ad-free use once as a Plus benefit and keep the stores' advertising/privacy declarations accurate.

Age and advertising choices are offered at startup through a dialog. Eligible adult Free riders may receive non-personalized or eligible limited ads when Google UMP permits requests. Refusal of optional consent does not automatically mean no ads; it can still permit limited ads. Under-18 and unknown-age users are excluded. Plus, unresolved entitlement and purchase-pending states suppress advertising. No interstitial, forced video or rewarded-ad proposition is part of the current offer.

The current production request policy waits at least 24 hours after the first confirmed-bike Home visit, limits new banner opportunities to three per session and six per rolling day, and spaces requests by at least 180 seconds, subject to stricter backend policy. These are implementation settings, not customer guarantees. A request, an empty slot and a served impression are different events. The UI keeps the loading/no-fill slot collapsed rather than presenting a fake ad.

Live units are configured. The last console inspection on 22 October showed limited serving and a request to link the public store listings and complete readiness review. Do not describe that dated observation as current approval or promise revenue/consistent fill once an app is approved. Store availability and AdMob readiness are separate operational steps.

First-party experience measurement is separately opt-in, off by default and account-linked, not anonymous. Assess task completion, retention and revenue together. In-app SDK use does not imply that an advertising SDK was added to the website. No provider settings are changed by this strategy update.

### Illustration only — not a forecast

Ad revenue = eligible free users × sessions/user × impressions/session × fill rate × realised eCPM / 1,000.

For illustration, 10,000 free users × 2 sessions × 2 impressions × 80% fill produces 32,000 served impressions. At hypothetical eCPMs of £1/£3/£6, that is £32/£96/£192 before other costs. These rates are sensitivity inputs, not measured UK motorcycle-app rates. Low-frequency utility use may generate little ad revenue.

At £3.99/month, 100 Plus subscribers produce £399 gross/month; 300 produce £1,197. Subtract applicable store charges/taxes, refunds, billing services, storage and support to obtain contribution. Do not mix gross subscription income with net advertising receipts as though both were profit.

Track monthly contribution: net ads + net subscriptions − variable servicing costs. Track fixed operating cost separately. Exclude hypothetical future garage revenue when assessing current runway.

### Why start at £3.99?

£3.99/month is the selected launch price, not validated willingness to pay. It reduces the commitment for an occasional-use ownership utility while keeping a paid contribution alongside the free audience and future garage business. Sell the concrete combination of no ads, up to 20 simultaneous bikes and custom reminders; do not rely on future features to justify today's price.

Compared with £4.99, £3.99 produces about 20% less gross revenue per subscriber. Matching the same gross revenue requires about 25% more subscribers, assuming the same billing duration and before cost differences. A conversion increase is a hypothesis, not a guarantee. Evaluate paid conversion, renewals/churn and net contribution per activated rider over several billing cycles. Review price or scope if paid users cost too much to serve; keep future attachment storage bounded. No annual plan or trial is included in the current launch offer.

## 12. Current garage enquiries and future garage SaaS

### Included now in the rider app

A Free or Plus rider can prepare a service plan/garage sheet, find nearby garages, choose a recipient and review an email enquiry before sending it through their own email app. Nothing is automatically broadcast or booked. The garage agrees availability, work, fitment, parts and price directly with the rider.

The lookup/map is a discovery tool, not a guaranteed partner directory. Do not promise every UK garage, a usable email address for every business or verified motorcycle expertise. A prepared email, a composer-reported send, delivery, a reply and a confirmed booking are separate outcomes. The app does not read replies or certify delivery.

### Founder-confirmed future: mobile tickets connected to a garage web app

The plan is to build reach through the rider app first, then introduce the garage SaaS. This is the intended second product phase, with timing and scope guided by rider demand and garage feedback.

1. **Build rider reach and repeat use.** Give riders useful Free ownership tools, grow the audience and learn which maintenance/service needs recur. Measure returning riders and genuine service preparation, not downloads alone.
2. **Turn mobile requests into garage tickets.** When a rider chooses a participating garage and submits a service request, send the selected bike details, requested work, relevant notes and rider-approved information into that garage’s web workspace as a structured ticket. The garage receives actionable context rather than having to re-enter an email or PDF.
3. **Give the garage a web application to manage the work.** The intended workflow is ticket received → garage review → clarification/quote → rider approval → agreed booking/work → progress → completion. Exact ticket states, screens and launch scope are still to be designed with garages.
4. **Keep both sides updated instantly.** Changes made in the garage web app should update the corresponding mobile ticket without the rider chasing by email or phone. Rider replies or approvals should likewise appear promptly in the garage workspace. Shared status, conversation and quote/approval updates are intended capabilities; reconnect/offline handling must be designed so the two sides do not silently disagree.
5. **Monetise recurring garage value.** Sell the garage workspace as SaaS once garages repeatedly use it and see value in handling rider enquiries and jobs. Validate pricing against the delivered workflow and actual willingness to pay.

The company’s longer-term proposition is therefore a connected rider-and-garage workflow: the mobile app builds the rider relationship and originates service tickets; the web app helps garages handle those tickets; timely updates keep both sides informed. A participating garage should receive only the information authorized for its request, with role-based access and an agreed shared record. A rider’s complete private maintenance history is not automatically exposed.

**Current versus future:** the submitted app currently prepares garage sheets and rider-sent email enquiries. Direct ticket delivery into a garage web workspace and instant two-way synchronization belong to this future phase. A listed business is not automatically a participating garage, and a submitted ticket is not automatically an accepted booking. Do not advertise this connected workflow as already available.

Use willing riders and a small garage pilot to establish useful ticket content, statuses, notifications and handling effort before expanding. The intended sequence is explicit; the dates, integration architecture, full feature set and garage subscription price remain open. Avoid building a broad garage-management suite before validating the core mobile-ticket-to-web workflow.

Keep outreach and rider enquiries separate. Riders review the recipient and selected data; do not attach their complete private record, contact multiple garages or append a software sales pitch without a distinct authorized purpose. Listing a garage does not make it a partner. No garage campaign is authorized by this document.
