# 2Shocks: launch and growth strategy

Strategy date: 7 September 2026. Workshop and pricing revision: 9 September 2026. Owner: founder. Review every two weeks during pre-launch and monthly after launch.

This is the working commercial strategy for the rider app and future garage product. It revises the earlier pricing and launch sequence. It does not change provider licences, data-retention rules, app-store products or backend entitlements by itself. Roadmap capabilities are proposals, not claims that the current app delivers them.

## 1. Goal and business thesis

Build a trusted UK motorcycle platform that makes ownership easy for riders and turns service enquiries into manageable, approved work for independent workshops.

The rider app launches first and must be useful without garage participation. Its excellent UI/UX should make motorcycle identification, history and service preparation easier than the fragmented alternatives. Rider adoption creates credibility and evidence for later garage recruitment. The garage SaaS is the intended principal recurring revenue business; the marketplace develops through participating workshops and explicit rider requests.

The commercial sequence is:

1. Earn rider trust with a useful standalone app.
2. Build an active audience concentrated around a reachable area.
3. Demonstrate local ownership and service interest to garages.
4. Introduce a small portal that works alongside existing workshop tools.
5. Prove bookings, reduced administration and paid retention.
6. Expand the workflow and marketplace only when the evidence supports it.

Do not require an unknown garage owner to replace their existing software on the first sale. Do not wait for a large national audience before doing lightweight workshop research.

## 2. Positioning and initial customer

### Rider launch promise

> Your bike’s history. Your next service. Together.

Start with your UK registration. Keep available MOT records and rider-entered maintenance together, and prepare a service plan you can show to any garage.

The first audience to investigate is UK owners aged 16 and over of used motorcycles who use independent workshops and have maintenance to organise in the next few months. Prioritise makes and models the app identifies reliably. Recent used-bike buyers are a promising entry point because their records and knowledge may be incomplete. Plus targets riders who want model-specific workshop reference, including single-bike owners. Multi-bike organisation is an additional benefit.

The app can be available nationally. Concentrate early community outreach and later paid tests in one geographic catchment. Choose that area in week 1 based on founder access, rider communities, relevant motorcycles and a reachable pool of workshops. No city has been selected yet.

### Garage proposition, later

> Review the bike, confirm the work, accept the booking. Less chasing from enquiry to completion.

Bring evidence of relevant local riders and service interest. Sell an easier workflow and useful enquiries, without guaranteed lead volumes. Let garages process their own existing customers as well as marketplace requests. Their customers should be able to review a quote through a simple web link without installing an app.

### Long-term rider proposition

> Get motorcycle work priced, booked and recorded with less effort.

The future journey connects motorcycle identity, guided questions, suitable nearby workshops, clear pricing, realistic availability, garage confirmation and completed service history. Use registration as the easiest UK starting point, with VIN confirmation where needed. Identification alone does not establish condition or diagnose a fault.

## 4. Revised offers and prices

These are proposed launch prices to test, not demonstrated willingness to pay. Consumer prices should be presented as the total payable and matched to the final store configuration before sale.

| Offer | Proposed price | Included value | Commercial role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2Shocks Free | £0 | One active bike; registration identification; available MOT information; live tax check; rider-entered maintenance history; basic service plans; suggested parts and shortlists | Adoption, trust and useful ownership context |
| 2Shocks Plus | £3.99/month or £29.99/year | Model-specific Workshop reference: available specifications, capacities, torque/adjustment data, service periods/tasks and repair information; all free features across up to five bikes | Useful technical reference even for a single-bike owner; recurring rider revenue |
| Garage research/pilot | No charge during a defined four-week operational pilot | A small agreed enquiry, quote and acceptance workflow, with manual onboarding | Establish usefulness and collect baseline/outcome evidence |
| Garage portal, initial paid test | £49/site/month, plus VAT if applicable | The validated narrow portal for enquiries, estimates, approvals and booking requests; scope confirmed before billing | Test willingness to pay without claiming full workshop management |
| Expanded garage workflow, later | Test £99/site/month, plus VAT if applicable | Only once delivered: integrated scheduling, job records, customer updates and completed history alongside the portal | Principal recurring revenue product |

The two garage prices describe stages, not two launch-ready public tiers. Agree pilot end date and proposed paid price before a pilot begins. Do not introduce automatic paid conversion or billing without explicit agreement. Do not grandfather an unlimited free service by accident.

### Why revise Plus?

The earlier £2.99/month and £24.99/year proposal assumed workshop reference was not included at launch. Workshop remains a planned core Plus benefit, conditional on a production agreement, and the founder selected £3.99/month and £29.99/year on 9 September 2026. Lead with the actual monthly price; show the full annual price and annual billing clearly beneath it. These replace all earlier proposed prices, including the briefly discussed £4.99/month and £39.99/year.

Workshop turns Plus into an offer for single-bike owners as well as collections. Sell the useful questions it answers: which lubricant specification, how much fluid, what a selected service period includes and which available adjustment or repair information applies. Provider API expense is an internal cost, not the customer proposition. The chosen price is not yet validated against usage or willingness to pay.

Basic intelligent parts matching remains in Free. Explain that resolved motorcycle identity guides catalogue suggestions and that shortlists can support a service plan. Do not imply guaranteed compatibility or automatic diagnosis.

Keep normal source-backed MOT and rider-scheduled reminders free where implemented and verified. Do not paywall maintenance history or basic service preparation. Keep eventual garage requests and quote approval accessible without a rider subscription. Avoid adding speculative premium benefits simply to fill a pricing card.

Workshop reference is planned for launch, subject to a signed production agreement and motorcycle/subject coverage. Do not add AI diagnosis, document storage, exports, transfer of ownership, unlimited lookups or advanced alerts until delivered and permitted. Exports or receipt storage may be evaluated later, but are not launch commitments.

Offer Plus after a successful bike record when the rider wants Workshop reference, or when adding another active bike. Track single-bike workshop interest and multi-bike conversion separately. Retain access to existing records on downgrade; define active-bike selection and archive behaviour before release.

### Economics before scale

Track net subscription receipts after applicable deductions, provider costs, support, hosting and acquisition costs. Provider cost includes identification, failed attempts, repeated refreshes and background activity across both free and paid riders. One active free bike does not itself limit repeated paid lookups.

Use permissible refresh controls and abuse protection. Never cache prohibited provider data to improve margins. Confirm supplier economics and licensing directly; no cost assumption in this document overrides an agreement.

Illustration only: 10,000 users with 3% paying £29.99/year produce £8,997/year, approximately £750/month in gross annualised rider subscription revenue. This does not establish that the free population is self-funding. Twenty garages at £49/month produce £980/month gross recurring revenue. Neither scenario is a forecast or a margin calculation.

### Paid-marketing budget and stop rules

These are proposed experiment caps, not authorised spending. No advertising accounts, campaigns or payments have been created.

- Weeks 1–8: £0 media spend by default. Founder-led recruitment and useful demonstrations first. Avoid buying traffic for disabled store links or an untested signup path.
- Months 3–4: after a hosted signup/download destination and measurement work, test £150–£300 total over two weeks in one catchment. Run one channel and two message variants. Set the cap before starting; no automatic expansion.
- Months 5–6: an optional £300–£600/month local experiment only if the first test produces activated riders and service intent at an affordable cost. If evidence is weak, return to organic recruitment and improve the product.
- Month 7 onward: no default spend commitment. Derive the next cap from runway, observed net contribution, acquisition cost and a founder-approved payback target. Do not count hypothetical future garage revenue as realised customer value.

Test messages: “Bought a used bike? Start its maintenance record” versus “Your MOT, maintenance and next service together.” Use real registration-to-record and service-planning clips. Avoid cheapest-repair promises, fear-based diagnosis claims and generic lifestyle ads that attract an irrelevant audience.

Primary acquisition metrics are cost per confirmed active bike, cost per useful service plan and geographic concentration. Cheap clicks, installs and email requests alone are insufficient. Stop a test at its cap, on broken attribution, or when it produces interest without useful activation. Small samples do not establish an optimal channel or price.

## 6. What to build now, next and later

### Now: standalone rider utility

Prioritise reliable registration identification, clear source/coverage states, a usable motorcycle record, quick maintenance entry and a service plan a rider can show on their phone. Parts are optional suggestions; creating a plan must not require the rider to diagnose the bike or select a part.

Preserve all established provider restrictions: no fabricated dates, mileage, legal status, maintenance intervals or fitment guarantees; no prohibited Haynes payload caching; no SORN inference from missing dates. Persist only data permitted by the relevant source and agreement. Keep rider-entered VIN and service information labelled accordingly.

The landing-page price change does not update RevenueCat, Apple/Google products or backend entitlement configuration. Align these before launch. Audit automatic reminders before advertising specific event delivery; switches and push registration do not prove that scheduled messages are sent.

### Next: a small garage portal

Start with incoming authorised enquiries, motorcycle context, garage-editable work and parts, versioned quotes, rider approval and clear acceptance. Avoid compulsory migration, full accounting, inventory fulfilment or an all-in-one workshop replacement.

Record baseline time spent on comparable enquiries before the pilot. Measure whether prepared context actually reduces work. Support enquiries from a garage’s existing customers, so value does not depend entirely on marketplace traffic. Integrate or export to existing workflows where that is necessary to avoid double entry.

### Later: price and booking confidence

Use three paths:

1. Known, well-defined work: garage-authorised upfront price when scope, model, parts and conditions are supported.
2. A repair with unresolved details: an estimate or range subject to explicit garage confirmation.
3. An unexplained fault: a priced diagnostic appointment, followed by approval for repair work.

Garages approve labour, parts policies/margins, consumables, taxes, scope exclusions and lead times. A price change requires rider approval before confirmation. Distinguish drop-off time from work-start or completion time. Parts readiness and real capacity affect bookability.

Treat “95% price certainty” as a research ambition, not public copy. Define the target before measuring it; for example, the share of supported jobs finishing within 10% of the initial estimate for unchanged scope. Also report scope-change frequency, exclusions and sample size. Q&A gathers useful evidence; it must not imply a universal remote diagnosis.

Only participating garages appear as bookable. Reviews must have a truthful source; no invented reviews or scraped unclaimed partnership claims. Preserve immutable sent quote versions and completed job records. New parts/pricing data must not silently rewrite old approvals. Repair payments stay with the garage initially; no transaction fee or payment-marketplace build is required by this strategy.

## 7. Measurement and decision rules

| Measure | Working definition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch request | A received explicit email request, deduplicated; a mailto click is not a request | Real permission to send the launch announcement |
| Bike activation | Rider confirms and saves a resolved motorcycle | Product proof moment |
| Useful activation | Within seven days, rider records maintenance, saves a service plan or saves a parts shortlist after confirmation | Value beyond a one-off lookup |
| Local active bike | Confirmed saved motorcycle with meaningful use in the previous 60 days, in a voluntarily supplied broad catchment | Relevant audience; count riders and bikes separately |
| Service intent | Plan with actual work/timing and optionally professional-help preference | Research and planning, not guaranteed demand |
| Authorised enquiry | Rider explicitly submits a request to a participating garage | A commercially actionable handoff |
| Retention | Cohort return at week 4 and month 3, plus return around a real maintenance/MOT event | Appropriate to an episodic ownership app |
| Garage adoption | Repeated real jobs handled, review time and staff usage | Operational value |
| Commercial proof | Explicit paid conversion, renewals, completed work and net contribution | Sustainable business |

Report unsuccessful identification and unsupported bikes separately; do not improve success rates by hiding failed lookups. Record reminder opt-in and actual delivery outcomes separately. Distinguish all-user Plus conversion, single-bike Workshop conversion and multi-bike conversion.

Show garages aggregated, dated local counts with definitions. Do not sell personal rider data or present inferred intent as a request. Broad location collection and any new analytics require appropriate product disclosure and privacy updates; these features are not installed by this website change.

Decision rules:

- Strong rider use, weak service intent: improve useful service preparation; do not claim a marketplace audience yet.
- Interest but weak repeat value: fix the rider product before buying more traffic.
- Garages like the idea but will not use it: inspect workflow friction and double entry; do not respond by building every requested feature.
- Garages use the portal but will not pay: investigate whether value, target segment, support burden or pricing is the issue before expanding.
- Garages pay without new marketplace leads: grow the proven SaaS workflow and let their existing customers help distribute the app.
- Both sides complete repeat work reliably: expand locally, then geographically.
